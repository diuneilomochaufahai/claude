## Summary

The closure rests on two defenses that the Department accepted without testing them against its own file: the owner's statement that he "did not know" about me, and the master tenant's statement that he "denied all allegations" and "complied" with advice to avoid me. The file contradicts both. The investigation also did not reach most of the allegations the Department itself confirmed were part of this complaint, did not analyze the disability accommodation claim at all, and reached a result that cannot be reconciled with the Department's own certification, signed July 21, 2026, that I was the victim of a sexual assault and of landlord retaliation on these same facts. I ask that the closure be rescinded and the case reopened and reassigned.

## Ground 1 — The owner's "no knowledge" defense was accepted although every act alleged against him occurred after he had written notice of me

The Response Review letter of August 19, 2026 summarizes Mr. Guo's position as: he "did not know about your occupancy and therefore felt no obligation to take any action." The investigator's email of September 16, 2026 adopted that position as the reason conciliation was "not viable."

My complaint against Mr. Guo is not about the years before 2026. It is about what he did after he knew:

- **May 4, 2026.** I wrote to his attorney, Enhan Cao, the only contact given on the May 1 notice, identifying myself by name as an occupant of Unit D since 2022 paying rent to Mr. Li. (File: my September 12 Guo packet, Exhibit 4; resent to the investigator September 21.)
- **May 14, 2026.** Written reasonable-accommodation request disclosing post-traumatic stress disorder and asking for four things, including "continued uninterrupted access to essential utilities." (Exhibit 5.)
- **May 18–19, 2026.** Water shut off, then gas and electricity. Written notice to Mr. Cao the same day. (Exhibits 6, 7, 8.)
- **June 16, 2026.** Written demand to restore water and to give the water district the owner confirmation it required. (Exhibits 8, 10.)
- **July 8, 2026.** Mr. Cao's court filing acknowledging my emails. (Exhibit 12.)
- **July 16, 2026.** Mr. Cao's email: Mr. Guo "only has obligations to provide accommodation to his tenant, Xiaolong Li, not others," and "has no duty to restore the utility service that Xiaolong Li has intentionally stopped"; settlement offered "the same as your proposal except the water service." (Exhibit 14.)

Notice to an agent is notice to the principal (Civ. Code § 2332). From May 4, 2026 the owner's knowledge was actual and in writing. Under FEHA the duties of an "owner" (Gov. Code § 12927(e)) run to "any person" (§ 12955(a)), not only to lease signatories, and a housing provider is directly liable for failing to correct a third party's discriminatory conduct that it knew of and had the power to correct (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, § 12010(a); 24 C.F.R. § 100.7(a)(1)(iii)). "I did not know" is not a defense to conduct after May 4. The closure letter does not say how the Department resolved this, and the September 16 email indicates it did not.

## Ground 2 — The failure-to-accommodate claim was never analyzed

The Department sent me a medical release on June 15, 2026; I returned it signed the next day. On June 17 the investigator wrote that the Department "will not be reviewing any of your medical records at this time." The August 19 summary contains no response from Mr. Guo to the May 14 request, and nothing in the file indicates that Mr. Guo or Mr. Cao was asked about it. The only response in the record is Mr. Cao's July 16 email: sixty-three days of silence followed by a categorical refusal, with no interactive process (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 2, §§ 12176–12178). A No Cause finding on a disability accommodation claim, made without verifying the disability, without asking the respondent about the request, and without addressing a written refusal, is not supported by the record.

## Ground 3 — As to Mr. Li, the Department applied the wrong standard and did not resolve the credibility conflict in its own file

The August 19 letter reports that Mr. Li "denied all allegations," that police "found no independent witnesses," and that he was "advised to avoid contact" and "complied." Three problems:

**(a) The absence of an eyewitness is not the test.** The file contains San Gabriel Police Report 2025-02394, taken within minutes of the September 2, 2025 incident, recording that "her landlord ... hit her once on the buttocks" and that the officer went back inside and told Mr. Li that a sexual battery report had been filed; my declarations of June 22 and August 3, 2026; and my account that Mr. Li gave inconsistent versions of the contact to the officers and to five housemates. (Li packet, Tabs 5 and 7; packet pp. 103–104, 167–168, 174–175.)

**(b) Mr. Li's statement that he "complied" with advice to avoid me is contradicted by recordings already in the file**: confrontations on September 15 and 23 and November 1, 2025; recorded exchanges on November 12, 2025 and January 4 and February 11, 2026; and bedroom-camera clips of April 24, May 5 and May 10, 2026 showing my door being opened while I was out and, on May 10, a man's head and upper body inside my room. (Li packet, Tabs 6, 8, 10–12; stills at packet pp. 81–91.) A civil harassment restraining order issued against him in LASC case 26PDRO00576 on [confirm date: May 14, 2026]. The investigation did not address this contradiction.

**(c) The Department never established what Mr. Li denied.** On August 27 and 28, 2026 I asked in writing whether he denied that any contact occurred or only its sexual character, because his accounts to the police and to the housemates differed. The question was not answered. Without knowing what was denied, the Department could not weigh his denial against the police report.

## Ground 4 — Allegations the Department confirmed were in the complaint were not investigated

On June 23, 2026, Supervisor II Michael Ehrgott wrote that the sexual harassment allegations against Mr. Li "are included in the HUD complaint you signed which is being investigated by CRD," and that additional allegations could be added through the investigator. I submitted a chronology and request to amend the same day. The August 19 summary nevertheless contains no response from Mr. Li to any allegation other than the September 2, 2025 incident, and the investigator's emails of August 31 and September 14 confirm that the retaliation and housing allegations were treated as outside the investigation.

Those allegations are documented in the file (Li packet, items 2 through 16) and include: the rent increase after the police report (October 8, 2025); the termination notice served two days after my first CRD complaint (February 23, 2026); the lockout of February 21–24, 2026; the misrepresentation of the City's April 17, 2026 notice as an order to vacate; the utility shutoff four days after my accommodation request; sole control of the mailbox; the entries into my room; and advertisements stating preferences by marital status, employment and immigration status. A No Cause determination cannot rest on allegations that were never put to the respondent.

## Ground 5 — The determination cannot be reconciled with the Department's own certification of July 21, 2026

On July 21, 2026, the Assistant Deputy Director of the Department's Appeals & Quality Assurance Unit signed Form I-918 Supplement B under this case number, certifying under penalty of perjury that I am or was a victim of sexual assault on September 2, 2025 in San Gabriel; that the "property manager sexually harassed her when he sexually assaulted her by smacking her on the buttocks without her consent"; and that "since then her landlord has engaged in discriminatory and retaliatory behavior, such as eviction notices, shutting off water, and shutting off the electricity." The certification states that it was made "based upon investigation of the facts" and that the case was "on-going." Ten weeks later the same facts were found to show no cause, in a letter that gives no reasons. I ask the Appeals Unit to explain how both determinations can stand and, if they cannot, to set the closure aside.

## Ground 6 — Investigative steps the file shows were not taken

- No interview of me after my September 12, 2026 rebuttal, which consisted of a cover letter, three analytical packets and a 207-page consolidated exhibit set with more than ten recordings; and no Cantonese-assisted interview at any time. The case closed fourteen business days after that rebuttal was uploaded.
- No indication that the Department obtained: SGPD report 2025-02394 and the body-camera footage of September 2, 2025; SGPD report 2026-00424 (February 21, 2026 assault and lockout); SGPD call 26-08976 (May 19, 2026); Detective Lara's file, including the bedroom recordings I delivered on June 5, 2026; the water district's stop-service form and account notes; the City of San Gabriel code enforcement file; the Guo–Li lease and the owner's communications with Mr. Li about the occupants and utilities; or statements from the housemates I identified.
- No interview of Mr. Guo or Mr. Cao about the May 14 request, the May 18–19 shutoffs, the June 16 demand, or the July 16 refusal. (Requested in my September 12 Guo packet, section 10.)
- Conciliation was abandoned on September 16 on the strength of the owner's "no knowledge" statement, without testing it against the May 4 letter already in the file.

## Ground 7 — No reasons were given

The closure letter states only "No Cause." I was not told which allegations were found unsupported, what legal standard was applied, or why the documents above were discounted. I request the written case analysis or determination memorandum with the appeal decision.
